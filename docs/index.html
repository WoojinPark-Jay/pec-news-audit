01 · Abstract

Abstract

Personalized news platforms are often evaluated as if stated preferences, logged recommendation exposure, and click consumption form a single coherent pipeline. Collapsing these layers can distort audit conclusions: a platform may appear more aligned or diverse than observed click consumption supports, which can misdirect diversity governance or algorithmic intervention. We introduce a reusable Preference–Exposure–Consumption (PEC) audit framework that separates stated preference, observed weighted profile state, logged recommendation exposure, app-surface pathways, and click consumption under explicit observability boundaries. Using six months of logs from a deployed mobile news application, we audit 1,583 user profiles, 95,143 logged recommendation items, and 17,512 click events. Each trace type contributes distinct information; none directly substitutes for another. Preference–consumption alignment exceeds chance-based null baselines but captures only part of users' top-consumed category set. Logged recommendation lists contain clicked articles more often than a date-matched candidate-pool baseline predicts (11.29% vs. 9.13%; top-5 lift 1.47×), but many clicks arrive through other app surfaces. Raw exposure–consumption diversity gaps shrink under count matching, yet concentration mismatch persists in the audit-eligible cohort (HHI gap 0.082). Together, the results show that audit conclusions change depending on whether platforms measure stated preference, logged exposure, or click consumption.

PEC Audit Protocol

Boundary: PEC is a measurement protocol. It is not a ranking objective, a causal estimate, or a production targeting procedure.

03 · Findings

Key Findings

The conclusions change with the layer being measured.

RQ1 · Preference

Stated preferences are informative, but incomplete.

The top consumed category appears in stated interests for 77.22% of eligible users, above chance-based nulls. Mean category Jaccard is only 0.228, showing that dominant interests do not capture the broader consumption set.

77.22% top-category match0.228 mean Jaccard

RQ2 · Traceability

Logged recommendation exposure carries signal, not complete attention.

Only 11.29% of comparable clicks appear in the same-day logged recommendation list, versus a 9.13% date-matched candidate-pool expectation. Top-5 lift reaches 1.47×, while most clicks arrive through other surfaces.

11.29% observed hit1.47× top-5 lift

RQ3 · Diversity

Diversity depends on the measurement layer.

Logged exposure is broader than click consumption in the raw logs. Count matching removes much of the entropy gap, yet the audit-eligible cohort retains an HHI gap of 0.082 and JS divergence of 0.471.

0.082 HHI gap0.471 JS divergence

RQ4 · Divergence

The gap is structured, but the signal is bounded.

Same-window diagnostic AUC reaches 0.859 with weighted profile-state features. It falls to 0.731 in the time-split diagnostic and below 0.71 in denser-history cohorts, limiting operational interpretation.

0.859 same-window AUC0.731 time-split AUC

Key takeaway

Preferences, logged recommendation exposure, interface pathways, and clicks are informative but non-substitutable traces. The apparent alignment and diversity of the same recommender changes with the layer being measured, so every audit claim should name its observable trace, eligible denominator, and interpretation boundary.
